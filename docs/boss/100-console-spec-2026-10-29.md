1. The master console is a FRONT DOOR with 5 menu items (Home, My games, Create, Billing, Account); it never runs or edits a game, it signs you in once, lists every game you have, starts one, shows what you owe, and hands you over to the right game.

## 3. DECISIONS (made in this judging; each is reversible until work starts)

### 3.1 The three designs, scored (1 = poor, 5 = best)
| | Super simple for customers | Complete (master + 5 games + Lucky Draw placeholder) | Reuse / least risk | Safety | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A. "Simplest for a non-technical HR user": ONE new shared 6-step console kit copied into all five games, Tambola migrated last | 5 | 4 | 2 | 3 | 14 |
| B. "Reuse-first, least risk": every game already has an older copy of Tambola's 10-step console; trim it, add a small shared run bar, leave live Tambola alone | 3 | 4 | 5 | 4 | 16 |
| C. "Safety, privacy, money": shared kit plus strict deploy/rollback/privacy rules, master Create asks Day/Time/Size itself | 4 | 4 | 3 | 5 | 16 |

Why B is the base: the owner said "similar lines of Corporate Tambola" and Tambola is live and earning; B changes the least live code (the four ports already contain Tambola's old console; Tambola's own 2,312-line zc.js is untouched, checked), and needs no rewrite across five repos on a laptop that has about 0.5 to 0.9 GB free RAM. A is the simplest to look at but is a rewrite of every game's console plus a Tambola migration, and its six-step shape would make Tambola and the others look different, against the order. C has the best safety list but its master Create wizard needs a fixed "golden payload" per game (a second wizard that must track five games' rules) and its shared kit is a rewrite.

# Legal and policy pages: These pages are written by the product team in plain language. They are drafts. A US adviser and a CA (India) must read them before the first paid launch. Nothing is deployed; the pages exist only in this folder tree.

Pages (all under `apps/web/public/`; the legal ones are generated by `tools/make-legal-pages.mjs`):
- `terms.html`: Terms of use for organizers and players (no fee to play, 18+, voluntary, any gift comes from the organizer outside this product, we hold no prize money).
- `dpa.html`: Data processing terms (the organizer is the controller of players' names, we are the processor; deletion within 24 hours of the end; backups 7 days; 365-day records).
- `subprocessors.html`: Cloudflare, Resend, Razorpay (through IT Services by Rajat Agarwal), Google sign-in. It still also names drand and YouTube, which this product uses only if an organizer adds a public beacon number or a video moment; the Tic Tac Toe console does neither, so the owner may drop both lines.
- `partner-terms.html`: Refer and earn terms (independent partner, no spam, tax, no commission without a buyer payment).
- `organizer-guide.html`: Rules for organizers (never ask players for a fee or a stake, check your state, tax on any gift).
- `legal.html` (Privacy Policy), `refund-policy.html`, `pricing.html`, `contact.html`, `about.html`: public information.

The data behind these pages is `docs/PRIVACY_AND_LIFECYCLE.md` (the data map) and `docs/RETENTION.md`. If a page and the data map disagree, the data map follows the code; fix the page.

Open questions:
1. Is a knockout tic tac toe tournament for a company, with no stake from players, no cash prizes and any gift supplied by the organizer, outside gambling and lottery rules in India and in each US state? The product never takes money from players and never holds or pays prize money. Winners are decided by the rules and a sealed seed (`docs/RULES.md`, R26 and R31), not by chance alone; the last-resort tie-break is a number from the seed (R10).
2. US state privacy laws (California and others) for employees as players.
3. Selling software from India to US customers: tax position and payout rules for US partners.
4. Razorpay: international cards must be approved on the account before US dollar sales.
5. DPDP: is a display name on a bracket, with a short notice and a tick box, enough consent? Must the short notice also name the 23-hour security log (network address, browser, device id)? See `docs/PRIVACY_AND_LIFECYCLE.md`, section 8.
6. Adults only (18+): is a tick box enough, given that the product asks for no date of birth and no proof?

These pages must not be published as final until confirmed.
